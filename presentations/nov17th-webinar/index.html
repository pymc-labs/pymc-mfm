Community context: establish where marketing mix modeling stands today. Skip the executive summary and key insights; move directly into the modeling problem.

The problem: contributions are latent, several specifications can explain the same history, and analysts have limited time and specialist expertise to evaluate their assumptions.

Our solution: shift repeated model development into shared pre-training. Introduce the vision for marketing inference across the scenarios represented by a knowledge-prior.

How does it work: define the knowledge-prior, simulate worlds with known truth, and train a transformer to infer structure and time-dependent contributions. Explain PFNs and flow matching, then introduce River as the first implementation.

The results: organize the evidence around decomposition under correct specification and misspecification, causal discovery, forecasting trade-offs, out-of-prior scenarios and inference speed. Distinguish demonstrated results from proposed evaluations; use verified benchmarks for any numerical claims.

Next steps: explain current limitations and the uneven boundary of the model's knowledge, then discuss research toward richer marketing and mediation systems.

How do we measure marketing?

Bayesian marketing mix models (Bayesian MMMs) are advanced statistical frameworks using Bayesian inference to quantify marketing channel effectiveness on sales, enabling marketers to make accurate, flexible, and robust resource allocation decisions.

How do we measure marketing? Bayesian marketing mix models, or Bayesian MMMs, are advanced statistical frameworks that use Bayesian inference to quantify how effective each marketing channel is at driving sales, so marketers can make accurate, flexible and robust resource allocation decisions.

The figure is illustrative. What we observe, y(t), is the sum of contributions we never see directly, here f1(t) and f2(t). Every peak in the total lines up with a peak in one component.

An MMM works backwards from the observed total to those contributions.

An infinite decision landscape

Building a regression model takes many iterations where someone scientifically tests how each change affects the model’s understanding of the world. Each model is a structural causal model and must be coherent with how the real world behaves.

Building a regression model takes many iterations, where someone scientifically tests how each change affects the model’s understanding of the world. Each model is a structural causal model and must be coherent with how the real world behaves.

The five equations are the same model. Each one highlights a decision the analyst has to make: the baseline, the additional covariates, the media effect, the noise and even the observed outcome.

Every choice changes the model; together they form an effectively infinite decision landscape.

Marketing Foundational Model

Observed inputs to graph-conditioned effects

This is the Marketing Foundational Model, from observed inputs to graph-conditioned effects. Click through the inference path first, then the training supervision.

Observed inputs (treatments, covariates, outcomes, time and masks, with unobserved outcomes masked) go into a shared encoder that applies causal time attention and variable attention and produces representations h.

A joint flow head models the effects and the graph together. A graph G sampled from it, together with the shared representations h, drives graph conditioning: graph-guided features plus an effect support mask. The conditional flow head then gives the effects for that graph, decomposed into base, treatment, covariate, latent and indirect components.

In training, simulation targets (graph G and effects d, used only for training) supervise three losses: a graph classification loss (edge cross-entropy) on the auxiliary classifier’s edge logits, plus a joint velocity loss on effects and graph and a conditional velocity loss on effects given the true graph, both over context and query windows. The true graph G conditions the features during training, and the auxiliary classifier does not select the graph.

Flow readouts require integrating the learned velocity field.

Transformers are not only for LLMs

Now, a transformer reads weekly sales and marketing covariates, using attention across time and variables, alongside sales history summaries and withheld-week markers. Doing so, it makes a representation of the dataset.

Now a transformer reads weekly sales and marketing covariates, using attention across time and across variables, alongside sales history summaries and withheld-week markers. In doing so it builds a representation of the dataset.

Each bi-attention block applies causal self-attention across time steps for each variable, then self-attention across variables at each time step, then a feed-forward network; every sub-layer is normalized and added back through a residual connection.

Click to add the graph-conditional flow. The encoded representations h feed a joint graph flow over effects and graph structure; a graph G drives graph-guided message passing and an effect support mask; the conditional flow decoder then gives the effects over time for that graph. Effects excluded by the graph are zero, and flow time is separate from observation time.

This is the whole workflow in code: from a CSV to posterior draws, in four steps. Each click types the next block.

01 Load model: from_pretrained loads the River-1 profile from the Hugging Face Hub. 02 Read the data: a weekly CSV with dates, sales, media spend and controls, split into history and the weeks to forecast. 03 Run inference: name the date, target, channel and control columns; the network conditions on the history without training its weights. 04 Read the posterior: inference returns an xarray DataTree, also stored as model.idata, with context and query windows.

Each posterior draw carries the channel contributions and the causal graph sampled with it. That is why the outcome is not a single number.

How do we evaluate?

  1. 1

    We generate a set of datasets that the network never observed, using ranges and seeds independent of those used during training.

  2. 2

    We test every dataset in different scenarios, such as hidden variables and fixed or varying baseline terms, running each scenario around five hundred times.

  3. 3

    We compare each result from the network against an oracle that receives the true model structure, a Bayesian regression (PathMC) and a frequentist least-squares regression (OLS).

  4. 4

    Every 6,000 training steps, we save a checkpoint, rerun all the comparisons and measure how each checkpoint improves or degrades in each condition.

How do we evaluate? We generate a set of datasets that the network never observed, using ranges and seeds independent of those used during training.

We test every dataset in different scenarios, such as hidden variables and fixed or varying baseline terms, and run each scenario around five hundred times. We compare each result against an oracle that receives the true model structure, a Bayesian regression (PathMC) and a frequentist least-squares regression (OLS).

Every 6,000 training steps we save a checkpoint and rerun all the comparisons, so we can see how each checkpoint improves or degrades in each condition. The oracle is a reference, not a competitor: it knows the true structure.

Open questions

  1. 1

    Even when recovery is demonstrated, the shape of effects such as carryover and saturation is not fully calibrated.

  2. 2

    The limits of extrapolation remain unclear: models can appear remarkably clever, yet behave foolishly when tested on data outside their training horizon.

  3. 3

    It is still unknown how the model behaves under structural breaks in the time series.

Open questions. One: even when recovery is demonstrated, the shape of effects such as carryover and saturation is not fully calibrated. Two: the limits of extrapolation remain unclear; models can appear remarkably clever, yet behave foolishly when tested on data outside their training horizon. Three: it is still unknown how the model behaves under structural breaks in the time series.

Marketing Foundational Models · 17 November 2026

Conclusions

  1. 1

    Within the learning horizon, the network decomposes better than traditional approaches such as linear regressions.

  2. 2

    Reduce repetitive model training to focus on understanding systems and their consequences. Test multiple assumptions and structures simultaneously, under uncertainty, without committing to just one.

  3. 3

    The current foundational model is an early-stage experiment and the smallest in our roadmap. Future networks, with a larger training corpus and more capacity, will offer greater capability and broader use-case coverage.

Conclusions. One: within the learning horizon, the network decomposes better than traditional approaches such as linear regressions. Two: reduce repetitive model training to focus on understanding systems and their consequences; test multiple assumptions and structures simultaneously, under uncertainty, without committing to just one. Three: the current foundational model is an early-stage experiment and the smallest in our roadmap; future networks, with a larger training corpus and more capacity, will offer greater capability and broader use-case coverage.
